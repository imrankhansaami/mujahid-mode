# The Mujāhid Mode Framework

> This is the developer overview. The full, standalone manuscript — with facilitation notes,
> worksheets, appendices, and publishing guidance — is in
> [`content/framework/mujahid-mode-framework.md`](../content/framework/mujahid-mode-framework.md).

> Discipline is not the absence of desire. It is the presence of a structure that outlasts the
> mood.

This document is the thinking underneath the app. The app is one implementation; the framework is
portable. You can run it on paper, in a notebook, or in any tool that lets you keep an honest
daily record.

---

## 1. The premise

Most people do not fail because they lack information. They fail because they are running a
**broken operating system**:

- **No definition of a winning day.** So every day is judged by mood.
- **No architecture.** The day is shaped by notifications, other people, and appetite.
- **No regulation.** The gap between urge and action is seconds long, and they have no pause.
- **No recovery.** One slip becomes a lost week because there is no protocol for returning.
- **No direction.** Discipline with no mission becomes vanity with a calendar.

Mujāhid Mode replaces those five gaps with five layers. Each layer answers a question the
previous one cannot.

| Layer | Question | Mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Who is doing this, and for whom? | Niyyah, contract, witness |
| Architecture | What does the day look like? | Prayer skeleton, blocks, planner |
| Regulation | What happens when the nafs speaks? | Pause protocol, gates, environment |
| Recovery | What happens after a fall? | Floor, never-miss-twice, return |
| Mission | What is all this for? | One project, weekly ship, 90-day horizon |

The order matters. Identity without architecture is sentiment. Architecture without regulation
collapses at the first urge. Regulation without recovery produces shame spirals. Recovery without
mission becomes maintenance. Mission without the rest becomes burnout.

## 2. Identity — the niyyah and the contract

**Principle:** you cannot build a new life on the identity of the old one, and you cannot sustain
an identity you have not defined.

The framework begins with three written artifacts:

1. **The niyyah** — who this is for, and why. Read again on day 60.
2. **The definition of a winning day** — behaviours, not outcomes. Measurable on a Tuesday when
   you feel nothing.
3. **The contract** — the rules of engagement, plus the name of one witness.

**Why it works:** intention converts ordinary acts into worship; a written standard converts
intention into something you can fail against and correct. Vague intentions produce vague days.

**Design rule:** the standard must be behavioural and small enough to repeat. "Get closer to
Allah" is beautiful and unmeasurable. "Five prayers on time, one page of Qur'an, one deep block"
is a day you can verify.

## 3. Architecture — the prayer skeleton

**Principle:** the prayers are not interruptions to the day. They are the architecture of it.

Most productivity systems ask you to fit worship around work. The believing day is built the other
way around:

```
Fajr      → opens the day (Qur'an, dhikr, one important thing)
Dhuhr     → breaks the day at its peak, re-centres it
Asr       → the afternoon reset
Maghrib   → closes the workday
Isha      → seals the day
```

Between the anchors, the framework places:

- **One deep block** — 60–90 minutes on the work only you can do, phone elsewhere.
- **One service block** — family, community, or the people you are responsible for.
- **One recovery block** — movement, food, rest that does not numb.
- **A buffer** — twenty minutes between blocks, so the plan survives contact with real life.
- **A shutdown** — capture open loops, choose tomorrow's three priorities, stop.

**Design rule:** three priorities, not ten. If it does not fit in the blocks, it does not fit in
the day. An unplanned day is an invitation.

## 4. Regulation — the nafs and the pause

**Principle:** you do not defeat an urge at the moment of temptation. You defeat it at the door.

The framework names the inner states honestly:

- **An-nafs al-ammāra** — the self that commands to evil; the impulsive appetite that says *now*.
- **An-nafs al-lawwāma** — the self that blames; the conscience that keeps turning the light on.
- **An-nafs al-muṭmaʾinna** — the self at rest; settled in obedience.

It then trains three skills:

### 4.1 Observation

Log the urge without acting on it or hating yourself for it. Time, place, feeling, response.
Patterns you cannot see will keep defeating you.

### 4.2 The pause

A rehearsed, six-step protocol: stop moving → breathe → name it ("this is an urge, not an
instruction") → change the body (wudu, water, movement) → delay ten minutes → log the choice.

### 4.3 The gates

Change the environment before the moment:

- Phone charges outside the bedroom.
- No phone for the first 30 minutes after waking or the last hour before sleep.
- Notifications off by default.
- The home screen is four apps, not forty.
- One screen-free block every day.
- A weekly digital sabbath window.

**Design rule:** willpower is a limited resource; environment is not. Make the right thing twenty
seconds easier and the wrong thing twenty seconds harder.

### 4.4 The four gates of desire

For the specific battle of lust, the framework uses four gates — the eye, the device, the hour,
and the room. The emergency protocol (HALT → change the body → change the input → name the truth →
log it) is rehearsed at low stakes so it works at high stakes.

## 5. Recovery — the floor and the two-day rule

**Principle:** consistency is not the absence of interruption. It is the speed of return.

Two mechanisms carry the whole system:

1. **The floor** — the smallest version of the day that still counts: five prayers, one page, one
   honest log, one small action. On a sick day, a travel day, a grief day, the floor protects the
   identity.
2. **Never miss twice** — one miss is information; two is a new identity. The second miss triggers
   the floor day, not a new start date.

**Why it works:** it removes the two most expensive stories — "I already failed, so I might as
well continue" and "I'll restart on Monday." Both turn a moment into a month.

**Relapse protocol:** stop the episode → admit it to Allah without excuses → tell one trustworthy
person → change one gate within the hour → return today, not Monday.

**Design rule:** the system measures returns, not perfection.

---

## 6. Mission — discipline with a direction

**Principle:** discipline that ends with you is a hobby. Discipline that serves others becomes a
legacy.

The final layer asks for **one** project, not a portfolio:

- It serves people beyond you.
- It uses your actual strengths.
- It can be started this week in a small form.
- It can be done with ikhlas.

The cadence is **one weekly deliverable** shown to one person. Small and real beats large and
imagined. After day 60, the horizon becomes 90 days: one outcome, one weekly ship, one witness,
one review.

## 7. The daily loop

```
Morning
  Fajr → Qur'an → dhikr → first important thing (before the phone)
Day
  Deep block → buffer → service block → Dhuhr/Asr anchors
Evening
  Recovery → Maghrib → family → Isha → shutdown → lights out
Throughout
  Log prayers, pages, guardrails, and one honest line
```

**The five commitments that never change:**

1. Five prayers.
2. A daily portion of Qur'an.
3. Dhikr.
4. One protected deep block.
5. Guarded attention (screen fast, lights out).

Everything else — the planner, the tools, the streak — serves these five.

---

## 8. The weekly loop

Twenty minutes, once a week:

1. Look at the numbers.
2. Name three wins.
3. Name **one** leak.
4. Choose **one** change for next week.
5. Make duʿāʾ and set the intention.
6. Show the review to your witness.

**Why one change:** the temptation is to fix everything on Sunday and collapse by Wednesday. One
change, protected, compounds.

## 9. The 60-day arc

| Week | Days | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1–7 | The Awakening — identity, niyyah, contract |
| 2 | 8–14 | Time as Sacred Trust — architecture, planner |
| 3 | 15–21 | The Disciplined Soul — systems, environment, floor · **Milestone I (day 21)** |
| 4 | 22–28 | Conquering the Nafs — observation, pause, anger, accountability |
| 5 | 29–35 | Lust Mastery — gates, emergency protocol, recovery |
| 6 | 36–42 | The Consistency Engine — never miss twice, review, seasons · **Milestone II (day 42)** |
| 7 | 43–49 | Legacy Execution — mission, weekly ship |
| 8 | 50–56 | Digital Purity + integration |
| 9 | 57–60 | The Return — final review, day-61 plan · **Milestone III (day 60)** |

Teaching days: three lessons per week (days 1, 3, 5 of each week). The days between are field
days: no new input, just the drills and the five commitments. Day 6 is the weekly review; day 7 is
integration — family, masjid, rest, a walk without a phone.

**23 lessons across 8 modules:**

1. **The Awakening** — The Gap · Who Is a Mujāhid? · The 60-Day Contract
2. **Time as Sacred Trust** — The Amanah of Hours · The Five Pillars of Your Day · The Barakah Planner
3. **The Disciplined Soul** — Motivation Is a Guest · Design the Default · The Minimum Viable Day
4. **Conquering the Nafs** — Know Your Nafs · The Pause Protocol · Anger, Envy, and Comparison · Accountability That Works
5. **Lust Mastery** — The Strongest Man · Guard the Gates · The Emergency Protocol · Recovery Without Collapse
6. **The Consistency Engine** — Never Miss Twice · The Weekly Review · Streaks, Seasons, and Sustainability
7. **Legacy Execution** — From Discipline to Mission · Ship Weekly and the 90-Day Horizon
8. **Digital Purity** — The Tool and the Trap

## 10. Measurement philosophy

The score exists to make the invisible visible, not to become the goal.

- **Prayers 50** (on time or jamāʿah 10, late 5, missed 0)
- **Qur'an 15** (9 for any reading, 15 at target)
- **Dhikr 10 · Deep work 10 · Screen fast 10 · Lights out 5**
- **Held = 70+**

The weights are a claim about priority: salah and Qur'an carry half the day. The score is a
mirror. When the streak makes you abandon the practice, drop the streak; keep the practice.

**Signals to watch, not obsess over:** consistency (days held / days lived), best streak, prayer
quality (jamāʿah/on time), Qur'an days, deep-work days, screen-fast days.

## 11. Adapting the framework

- **30 days:** compress to one module per week and keep the five commitments. Drop the mission
  layer if needed.
- **90 days:** run the 60 days, then a 30-day mission sprint: one outcome, one weekly ship.
- **Ramadan:** use the compressed mode — prayers, Qur'an, one thing. Add tarāwīḥ as the evening
  anchor and protect sleep.
- **Travel / illness / newborn:** the floor only. Never zero.
- **Students:** deep block in the morning; service block becomes study or family; recovery block
  is non-negotiable.

The framework is a skeleton. The five commitments are the bone; everything else is muscle you can
rearrange.

## 12. References and caution

The framework draws on the Qur'an and the Sunnah for its orientation, and on modern behavioural
design (cue–routine–reward, implementation intentions, friction, environment design) for its
mechanics.

Qur'anic translations are paraphrased for readability and cited by surah and ayah. Hadith are
attributed where a collection is known; wording and gradings vary. This is not a fiqh manual. For
worship, rulings, and anything binding, return to the Arabic, the scholars, and a qualified
teacher.

If a behaviour feels compulsive — especially in the area of lust — treat it as a health issue and
seek qualified help. Taking the means is part of tawakkul, not a lack of faith.
