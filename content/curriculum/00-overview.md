# How to run the sixty days

> This is not a course you will get to later. It is a day-by-day structure that tells you what to
> do next — especially on the days you feel nothing.

## What this is

Mujāhid Mode is a 60-day discipline curriculum for the Muslim who already knows what he should be
doing and is tired of not doing it. It combines:

- **8 modules** — identity, time, systems, the nafs, desire, consistency, legacy, and attention.
- **23 lessons** — short readings (5–8 minutes) with drills and reflection prompts.
- **60 days** — one focus per day, three teaching days per week, and field days in between.
- **3 milestones** — days 21, 42 and 60 for honest review and consolidation.
- **A daily system** — five prayers, Qur'an, dhikr, one deep block, guarded attention.

It is a practical synthesis, not a reproduction of any paid course. It is also not a fiqh manual or
a substitute for a scholar, a therapist, or a doctor.

| 6 | 36–42 | The Consistency Engine | Two-day rule, review, seasons · **Milestone II** |

| 9 | 57–60 | The Return | Final review, day-61 plan · **Milestone III** |

Teaching days are **days 1, 3 and 5 of each week**. The days between are **field days**: no new
input, just the drills and the five commitments. Day 6 is the **weekly review**. Day 7 is
**integration** — family, masjid, rest, a long walk without a phone.

## Before day one

Do these four things in one sitting, before the first lesson:

1. **Write the niyyah.** One paragraph: who this is for, and why. You will read it on day 60.
2. **Define a winning day** in behaviours, not outcomes. Example: five prayers, two pages of
   Qur'an, one deep block, phone out of the bedroom.
3. **Write your floor** — the smallest day that still counts. Five prayers, one page, one honest
   log, one small action.
4. **Name one witness.** A trustworthy person who may ask you three specific questions each week.

The printable pack contains a one-page contract for this. Fill it in by hand and keep it where you
will see it.

## The daily loop

```
Morning
  Fajr → Qur'an → dhikr → the first important thing (before the phone)
Day
  Deep block → buffer → service block → Dhuhr and Asr as anchors
Evening
  Recovery → Maghrib → family → Isha → shutdown → lights out
Throughout
  Log prayers, pages, guardrails, and one honest line
```

**The five commitments that never change:**

1. Five prayers.
2. A daily portion of Qur'an.
3. Dhikr.
4. One protected deep block.
5. Guarded attention — screen fast, phone outside the bedroom, lights out.

Everything else serves these five. The planner, the trackers, the streak — all of it is scaffolding.

## The weekly loop

Twenty minutes, once a week. Use the weekly review sheet.

1. Look at the numbers.
2. Name three wins.
3. Name **one** leak.
4. Choose **one** change for next week.
5. Make duʿāʾ and set the intention.
6. Show the review to your witness.

**Why one change:** the temptation is to fix everything on Sunday and collapse by Wednesday. One
change, protected, compounds.

## How you are measured

The daily score is out of 100:

| Component | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Five prayers | 50 (on time or jamāʿah 10 each; late 5; missed 0) |
| Qur'an | 15 (9 for any reading; 15 at target) |
| Dhikr | 10 |
| Deep work | 10 |
| Screen fast | 10 |
| Lights out | 5 |

A day is **held** at 70. The weights are a claim about priority: salah and Qur'an carry half the
day. The score is a mirror, not a master. If the streak ever makes you abandon the practice, drop
the streak and keep the practice.

Track these signals, not just the total:

- consistency (days held ÷ days lived)
- prayer quality (on time and jamāʿah)
- Qur'an days (not only pages)
- deep-work days
- screen-fast days
- the quality of the weekly review

## The three milestones

| Milestone | Day | The question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I — The First Gate** | 21 | What has actually changed? What is still theatre? |
| **II — The Midpoint** | 42 | Which leak am I protecting, and what one change carries me to day 60? |
| **III — The Return** | 60 | Read the day-one contract. Write the day-61 plan. What do I keep, drop, start? |

Milestone reviews are not celebration pages. They are honest accounting pages. The printable pack
has a two-page milestone review for all three.

## Running it with a group

If you are facilitating this for a small circle:

- **One cohort, one pace.** Do not let people jump ahead on the lessons; the sequence is the
  curriculum.
- **Weekly circle (45–60 minutes).** Three questions: What did you actually do? Where did you
  slip? What is your one change? Keep it specific.
- **Pairs, not confessionals.** Assign a witness pair. Do not ask people to disclose the private
  log in public.
- **Privacy is part of the design.** The lust mastery material is deliberately private. Never
  require disclosure of it.
- **No shame mechanics.** The facilitation script is "return fast", not "prove you are serious".
- **Scholar and professional boundaries.** Bring fiqh questions to a qualified teacher. Bring
  compulsive behaviour to a qualified counsellor.

## Adapting the programme

- **30 days:** one module per week, keep the five commitments, drop the legacy module if needed.
- **90 days:** run the 60 days, then a 30-day mission sprint: one outcome, one weekly ship.
- **Ramadan:** compressed mode — prayers, Qur'an, one thing. Add tarāwīḥ as the evening anchor and
  protect sleep.
- **Travel, illness, newborn:** the floor only. Never zero.
- **Students:** deep block in the morning; service block becomes study or family; keep the recovery
  block.

The skeleton is fixed. The muscle is yours to arrange.

## A note on sources and safety

Qur'anic translations in this curriculum are paraphrased for readability and cited by surah and
ayah. Hadith are attributed where a collection is known; wording and gradings vary. For worship,
rulings, and anything binding, return to the Arabic, the scholars, and a qualified teacher.

If a behaviour feels compulsive — especially in the area of lust — treat it as a health issue and
seek qualified help. Taking the means is part of tawakkul, not a lack of faith.

*Move with niyyah, not hesitation.*
